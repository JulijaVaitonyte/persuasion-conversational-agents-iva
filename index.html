About the workshop

Conversational agents are increasingly agreeable, empathetic, and encouraging. These qualities can make them easier to interact and cooperate with, but they may also make agents persuasive or even manipulative.

This workshop will bring together researchers from socially interactive agent design and evaluation, machine learning, AI safety, cognitive psychology, neuroscience, sociology, and business and marketing. Through an invited keynote, pre-workshop reflections from participants, and roundtable discussions, participants will identify key challenges, research directions, and methodological building blocks for studying persuasion that emerges through social interaction with conversational agents.

The workshop will connect ideas shared by registered participants before the event with in-person discussion at IVA 2026. Its central goal is to co-construct a shared research agenda for studying persuasive and potentially harmful patterns in human–agent interaction.

Workshop goals

  • Define priority research questions for persuasion in conversational agents.
  • Discuss concepts, measures, tools, and evaluation frameworks.
  • Bridge perspectives across technical, behavioural, and social sciences.
  • Support future collaborations and a possible post-workshop publication.

Questions guiding the workshop

Discussions will be shaped by participant submissions and will build on questions such as:

  • How can persuasion be studied beyond short-term opinion shifts, including effects on real-world behaviour?
  • How should manipulation, deception, and conversational “dark patterns” be defined and operationalised?
  • How can long-term interaction effects be studied while conversational-agent technologies continue to change rapidly?
  • Which subjective and objective measures are needed, and how should they be combined?
  • How can work on persuasion connect with AI safety, responsible AI, behavioural science, and socially interactive agents?

Invited keynote

Keynote speaker

Dr Pat Pataranutaporn

Assistant Professor of Media Arts and Sciences, MIT Media Lab

Dr Pat Pataranutaporn is the founding director of the Cyborg Psychology research group and co-director of the MIT Media Lab’s Advancing Humans with AI research programme. His work brings together artificial intelligence, human–computer interaction, and psychology to investigate how human–AI systems can support—or diminish—learning, decision-making, critical thinking, creativity, and wellbeing. His research includes empirical work on emotional dependence on AI companions, cognitive manipulation, false-memory formation, and the broader effects of AI on human cognition, emotion, and behaviour.

Participation

The workshop is intended for researchers at all career stages, from early-career researchers to established scholars.

Participation does not require a paper submission. Prospective participants will be invited to register and, as part of registration, share a brief question, challenge, example, or methodological idea that they would like the workshop to address. These contributions are prompts for collective reflection—not formal submissions—and will not be peer reviewed or published as proceedings.

Help shape the conversation: visit the participation page to learn what you can contribute before the workshop and how those ideas will inform the roundtable sessions.
