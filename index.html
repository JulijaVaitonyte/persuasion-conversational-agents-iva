About the workshop

Humans generally prefer agents that are agreeable, empathetic, and encouraging, which are the characteristics increasingly exhibited by LLM-based Conversational agents (CAs). However, sociality is a double-edged sword. The same qualities that make CAs for humans easy to co-operate with, also make them persuasive and even manipulative. Despite the advanced social capabilites of CAs, there are no standardized evaluation approaches for studying persuasion in human-agent interactions, as well as measuring persuasive and potentially harmful interaction patterns.

The workshop is intended for researchers at all career stages, from early-career to established researchers, working across the fields of Socially Interactive Agent design and evaluation, machine learning, AI safety, and cognitive psychology, among others. Through an invited keynote, pre-workshop reflections, and roundtable discussions, participants will identify key challenges, research directions, and methodological building blocks for studying persuasion that emerges through social interaction with CAs.

The workshop will connect ideas shared by registered participants before the event with in-person discussion at IVA 2026. Its central goal is to discuss a shared research agenda for studying persuasive and potentially harmful patterns in human–agent interaction.

Workshop goals

  • Define priority research questions for persuasion in CAs.
  • Discuss concepts, measures, tools, and evaluation frameworks.
  • Bridge technical and social science perspectives.
  • Discuss future collaborations and a possible post-workshop publication.

Questions guiding the workshop

Discussions will be shaped by workshop contributors’ advance reflections and will build on questions such as:

  • How can persuasion in CAs be studied beyond short-term opinion shifts, particularly in terms of real-world behaviour and impacts on humans?
  • To what extent can we talk about “dark patterns” in CAs?
  • How can we operationalize concepts such as manipulation, deception, or “dark patterns” in CAs, and how do they relate to research conducted in the fields of AI Safety and Responsible AI?
  • How can long-term interaction effects be studied while conversational-agent technologies continue to change rapidly?
  • What subjective (e.g., survey-based) measures, whether existing or newly developed, are needed to study persuasion in CAs, and how to best combine them with more objective measures (e.g., neurophysiology)?

Invited keynote

Keynote speaker

Dr Pat Pataranutaporn

Assistant Professor of Media Arts and Sciences, MIT Media Lab

Dr Pat Pataranutaporn is the founding director of the Cyborg Psychology research group and co-director of the MIT Media Lab’s Advancing Humans with AI research programme. His work brings together artificial intelligence, human–computer interaction, and psychology to investigate how human–AI systems can support—or diminish—learning, decision-making, critical thinking, creativity, and wellbeing.
