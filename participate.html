How participation works

This workshop is designed as a collaborative forum rather than a traditional paper workshop. Anyone interested in persuasion and social influence in conversational agents is welcome to register, subject to the attendance and registration arrangements of IVA 2026.

During registration, we plan to invite you to share a short reflection—approximately 100–250 words—on an issue that you believe the community should discuss. This might be a research question, a conceptual difficulty, an example from your work, a methodological need, or a concern that existing research has not yet addressed.

These reflections are not formal submissions. They will not be peer reviewed, ranked, archived, or treated as conference publications. Their purpose is to help participants arrive with ideas already in mind and to help the organisers identify themes for the roundtable sessions.

What could you contribute?

Your reflection can be exploratory. Work in progress, unresolved questions, and cross-disciplinary perspectives are especially welcome.

  • A persuasive interaction pattern that current studies or benchmarks fail to capture.
  • A question about where persuasion ends and manipulation, deception, or a conversational dark pattern begins.
  • A challenge in studying behavioural or psychological effects over repeated or long-term interaction.
  • A subjective, behavioural, computational, or physiological measure that could improve evaluation.
  • A concrete case from healthcare, education, companionship, marketing, decision support, or another application area.
  • A methodological or ethical tension that should be reflected in a shared research agenda.

Suggested registration prompt

What question, challenge, example, or methodological idea should the workshop address when developing a research agenda for persuasion in conversational agents?

Please answer in approximately 100–250 words. You may also indicate which guiding question or application area your contribution relates to.

How your input will be used

Before the workshop, the organisers will group the reflections into broad themes and use them to refine the discussion prompts and roundtable structure.

We may summarise recurring themes during the workshop, but we will not publicly attribute or reproduce an individual contribution without permission. The resulting discussion will feed into a collaboratively developed research agenda and may support future research collaborations, including an optional post-workshop publication.

Important: registering your interest or sharing a reflection does not constitute a publication, an accepted paper, or a commitment to join a later paper. Any post-workshop collaboration will be discussed separately and participation will be voluntary.

Who should participate?

We welcome researchers at all career stages working in socially interactive agents, conversational AI, human–computer interaction, machine learning, AI safety, responsible AI, cognitive psychology, neuroscience, sociology, and business or marketing, as well as adjacent areas.

You do not need to identify primarily as a persuasion researcher. Perspectives from researchers studying trust, reliance, social influence, companionship, decision-making, behaviour change, agent evaluation, or human flourishing are highly relevant.
